You already know this from A-level. A **probability** is a number between 0 and 1. If you could repeat an experiment many times, it is roughly the fraction of times the event happens. The probabilities of all the possible outcomes add up to 1.

$$P(\text{rolling a six}) = \tfrac{1}{6}$$

On these pages, $P(\cdot)$ is a probability of an event, and the same letter $p$ or $q$ in lower case is used for whole distributions. Everything else in the probability family is built on top of this.
