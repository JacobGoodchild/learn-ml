---
id: integration
title: Integration as area
summary: The integral of a function between two points is the area under its graph.
colour: calculus
level: 0
---

From A-level Maths:

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x = \text{the area under } y = f(x) \text{ from } x = a \text{ to } x = b$$

(with area below the axis counting as negative). You can think of it as adding up lots of thin strips of width $\mathrm{d}x$ and height $f(x)$.

In probability, integrals turn densities into probabilities: the probability of landing between $a$ and $b$ is the area under the density curve between them. See [probability density](c:probability-density).
