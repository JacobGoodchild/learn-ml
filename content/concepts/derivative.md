From A-level Maths: $\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}$ is the gradient of the curve $y = f(x)$ at a point: how much $y$ changes per unit change in $x$, for a tiny change. Positive means going uphill, negative means downhill, zero means a flat spot such as a peak or a valley.

Useful ones here: $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}\log x = \frac{1}{x}$, $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}e^{x} = e^{x}$, and the chain rule $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}f(g(x)) = f'(g(x))\,g'(x)$.

A function of many inputs has a separate rate of change for each input: see [partial derivatives](c:partial-derivative).
